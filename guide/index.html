Install it, cut your first edit in five minutes, then learn every tab.

DROP IT! is a Premiere Pro panel that listens to the music on your timeline and helps you cut on what you hear: kicks, snares, hits, builds and drops. This guide covers version 0.9.7 (beta).

Installation

Installing takes about a minute: double-click the .ccx file, confirm one Adobe window, open the panel in Premiere.

  1. Download the .ccx file from Gumroad. Pay what you like, $0 works.
  2. Close Premiere Pro if it is open. It is not strictly required, but it saves a restart later.
  3. Double-click the .ccx file. The Creative Cloud app opens and installs the plugin into Premiere Pro. If nothing happens, open Creative Cloud, sign in, and double-click the file again.
  4. Confirm the Adobe window shown below by clicking Install.
  5. Open the panel in Premiere: Window › UXP Plugins › DROP IT!. Dock it under or next to the Program Monitor.

Quick start: a first edit in five minutes

The panel opens in Simple mode, with three tabs: Music, Edit and FX. That is all you need for a first cut.

  1. Put the music on the timeline, on an audio track of your sequence.
  2. Put your clips on the timeline too, on V1, in any order and any length. In Simple mode DROP IT! cuts from the clips on the timeline. These raw clips become the edit: what is left of them outside it is removed, and Undo brings them back.
  3. Select the music clip (with only one audio track you can skip this) and press Analyze in the panel. It takes a few seconds and is remembered: the same music file never needs a second analysis.
  4. Watch the live view. Press play in Premiere: the lamps flash on every kick and snare, and the big word at the top names the part of the song (INTRO, GROOVE, BUILD, DROP).
  5. Open Edit and pick a style: Super calm, Calm, Balanced, Driving or Hype.
  6. Press SURPRISE ME! Your clips are cut to the music on V1.
  7. Not quite right? Press Another version for a different edit with the same style, or the Undo arrow in the top bar to go back.
  8. Optional: Add FX on the drops. Empty adjustment layers land on the drops and big hits; drop your own effect on them. You need one adjustment layer in the project first: Project panel › New Item › Adjustment Layer.

Want more control: the clip source, bursts, checking your own cuts? Switch the top bar to Pro.

Analyze

Press Analyze with the music clip selected (nothing selected: the longest audio clip is used). A three-minute track takes about ten seconds the first time; after that the result is remembered for that file. The folder icon next to Analyze reads an audio file from disk instead.

The analysis finds the tempo, the beat, the parts of the song (intro, groove, build, drop and others) and the accents in nine layers: BEAT, KICK, BASS, SNARE, HI-HAT, PERC, HITS, CHORDS and a CUSTOM frequency band you set.

The top bar

  • Analyze, then the folder icon to analyze a file instead
  • Status line: what just happened, or what a greyed-out button is waiting for
  • Tempo with ½× and 2×. Whether a slow song is 67 or 134 BPM is a matter of feel; these keys count it at half or double. The beats stay on the same hits
  • Simple / Pro (see below)
  • Live sync (the pulse icon): on, the panel follows Premiere's playhead and moves it when you click in the panel. Off, it never touches the playhead; every button still works
  • Undo last action: takes back the plugin's last change, up to 8 steps
  • Settings (the gear): panel size, keys, reset, and the Log

The live view

  • The section bar across the top shows the energy of the whole song as one line, with its parts named underneath. Click or drag on it to move the playhead.
  • The header names the part of the song you are in (GROOVE, BUILD, DROP), what to cut on there, the clock and how long until the next part.
  • The lanes show one layer each. Every mark is an accent; the lamp next to the name flashes when the playhead passes one. The music's waveform runs faintly behind all lanes.
  • PLAN shows where the plugin would cut; EDIT shows your own cuts after Check; BURSTS and FX show where those would go.
  • Focus shows only the lanes that matter in the current tab. Click "Focus · 4 more" or press L to see all of them.
  • S next to a lane solos it. Drag in the lanes to scrub; click a mark to jump to its frame.

Premiere does not let a plugin start or stop playback, so Play stays in Premiere: click once in the timeline or the Program Monitor, then press the space bar.

Simple and Pro

The panel starts in Simple: three tabs (Music, Edit, FX) and the few choices that matter for a first cut. Pro shows every tab and every control: Bursts, Check, Match, the clip source, fine-tuning. Switching only hides controls; it never resets them. Once you choose Pro, the panel stays in Pro.

The plugin suggests, you decide

DROP IT! never takes over your edit. It proposes: a plan of cuts, places where a burst fits, cuts that are a few frames off. You apply what you like.

  • Everything the plugin puts on the timeline is one Undo away, up to 8 steps back, within one session of the panel.
  • Your own clips, effects and adjustment layers are never changed. The plugin only replaces what it placed itself.
  • Snap trims cuts; it never moves the picture inside a clip.

Every tab in detail

The tabs follow the order of work: Music, Edit, Bursts, FX, Check, and Match as an extra at the end. Simple mode shows Music, Edit and FX.

1 Music: Layers

One strip per layer: BEAT, KICK, BASS, SNARE, HI-HAT, PERC, HITS, CHORDS and CUSTOM.

  • The dot shows or hides a layer in the live view.
  • The fader sets how many marks a layer has: left keeps only the strongest, right keeps more.
  • CUSTOM is a frequency band you type in (for example 300 to 1200 Hz). Use it for a sound no other layer catches.
  • M includes the layer in Add markers.
  • Add markers writes coloured markers for the layers marked M, and replaces the markers it added before.
  • On Sequence / Music clip: Sequence puts them on the timeline ruler. Music clip puts them on the music clip itself, so they move with it.
  • Clear markers removes the plugin's markers from both places. Your own markers stay.

1 Music: Your sound

For the sound the built-in layers miss: a sampled kick under a rumble, a vocal chop, a cowbell, a riser hit. You pick it by ear, the plugin finds every repeat, and it becomes a lane of its own.

  1. Stop playback and put the playhead just before the sound.
  2. Press Play: the panel plays 0.6 s from the playhead.
  3. Pick the band where you hear the sound most clearly: All, Bass (below 150 Hz: kick, 808), Low (150 to 500 Hz), Mid (500 Hz to 3 kHz: snare, claps, vocals) or Air (above 3 kHz: hi-hats, cymbals). Or drag a box around the sound on the spectrum.
  4. Press Find this sound (key F). It finds the first clear hit and every moment it comes back.
  5. Walk through the results with Next (key N). Not this (key X) drops a wrong one, + Example adds one it missed.
  6. Match sets how close a repeat must be. Your sounds are saved with the analysis of that music file, up to four per file.

2 Edit

Three steps, closed by SURPRISE ME!: Style (how the edit moves), Clips (what goes in), Make (what changes and how).

1 Style

  • Super calm, Calm, Balanced, Driving, Hype: from long shots with no rush to as fast as the music allows. Super calm suits children's music, acoustic songs and a first dance: long shots, no bursts.
  • Speed fine-tunes the style. Holds sets how long the edit holds on a drop and how much the quiet and loud parts differ.
  • Cut before the hit, from -3 to 4 frames: 1 reads as on the beat, 0 is exactly on the hit, a negative number cuts after it.
  • The three numbers underneath say what the style means for this track: the shot length where it is quiet, where it grooves, where it peaks.

How an edit is built: the music is read in phrases, usually four bars. Held shots go first, at the start, on every drop and at the end. Between them the pace follows the energy and changes only where the music changes.

2 Clips

  • Source: the clips on the timeline, the Project panel selection, or a bin. Only real footage is used; titles and mattes are left out.
  • Order (Random or In order), Part (where in a clip a shot starts) and Each clip once.
  • Opening and ending: Hold gives the first and last shot time to register, Half makes them half as long, Off treats them like any shot.
  • Make: Clips or Empty slots. Empty slots puts one coloured placeholder per shot, to judge a rhythm before any clip is in. Fill slots then fills them from the Source.

3 Make

  • Selected shots get New clips or New cuts. Select shots on the timeline and press SURPRISE ME!: only those change. New clips keeps the cuts and swaps the clips; New cuts cuts the selected stretch again.
  • Bursts in the edit: Suggest (the edit has none; the Bursts tab shows where they fit), Auto (SURPRISE ME! puts them in), Off.
  • Version: every edit has a number. The arrows walk through the versions you made; type a number and press Run to make that exact edit again.
  • Fine-tune: Variation (how far a version may move from the plan), what changes (Rhythm, Clips) and a wished number of shots.
  • Every press of SURPRISE ME! makes a new version on the same track. Clip sound goes to its own muted audio track; the music is never touched.

3 Bursts (Pro)

A burst is a run of very short shots into a hit, then a held shot. The plugin suggests where bursts fit this music; you choose the look, the place and the flash. In SURPRISE ME! at the top is the same switch as Bursts in the edit: Suggest, Auto or Off.

2 Where

  • The preview on top shows what the picked suggestion would do: the shots before it, the burst, the held shot, and white ticks for flash frames.
  • The list under it has up to six suggestions, each with its time and reason: a run-up into a drop, a lift, a snare fill, a hi-hat roll. Click one to pick it; the playhead jumps just before it.
  • Apply puts the picked burst on the timeline. Apply all puts every suggestion in at once; one Undo takes them all back.
  • Burst here makes a burst at your own spot: into the strongest hit near the playhead, or inside the shots you selected.
  • Bursts work on clips and on empty slots.

3 Flash

  • Off, White, Black, Overexpose, Invert, B&W. Flash frames go on their own track above the edit, so the clips underneath stay whole. Move them to change the rhythm.
  • White and Black are clips the plugin brings into a DROP IT! bin. Overexpose, Invert and B&W are copies of your adjustment layer with the effect on, so they need one adjustment layer in the project.
  • In a vertical sequence the copies are scaled to cover the whole frame.

Fine-tune: Shape (Even, Faster, Slower), Speed (Auto, 8th, 16th, Triplet), Length (half a bar to two bars), Cut on (the beat grid or a layer), Clips (Nearby, One clip, Stutter, Any), flash length (1 to 4 frames) and which cuts flash.

4 FX

FX places empty adjustment layers on the strongest moments of the music. You drop your own effects on them: a glow, a shake, a zoom.

  • First, one adjustment layer in the project: Project panel › New Item › Adjustment Layer. Until there is one, the tab shows how to make it. In a non-English Premiere, use "My adjustment layer has another name" and select yours.
  • Add FX layers places copies of it on the drops first, then the strongest moment of each part, then the strongest anywhere.
  • Layers, Shortest, Longest: how many layers and how long, in frames. Type a number or use the small arrows.
  • FX track: Auto uses the plugin's own track above the picture, or pick one. Your own adjustment layers are never touched.
  • Inside held shots adds short layers inside long shots, on the beat, so a held shot stays alive. Centred on the hit puts the middle of each layer on the hit.
  • The list shows where the layers go and why, in Premiere's timecode. Untick a line to leave it out. After placing, it shows what is on the timeline now.
  • Remove at playhead takes away only the plugin's layer under the playhead. Remove all takes away every layer the plugin placed, on any track.
  • Layer at playhead: one empty adjustment layer centred on the playhead, Frames each side before and after it (3 gives 6 frames). It needs no analyzed music.
  • Once FX layers are on the timeline, every new SURPRISE ME! moves them onto its new cuts.

5 Check (Pro)

For an edit you cut yourself: cut by feel, then let the plugin tighten your cuts onto the music. Two steps.

  1. Check my edit reads the cuts on your video track and changes nothing. For each cut it finds the nearest strong accent: a hit, a kick, a snare, a bass note, a chord change or the beat. The four tiles count them: On an accent (lime), To move (amber), Stay (nothing strong close enough) and Gaps. The EDIT lane shows the same.
  2. Snap moves every amber cut onto its accent. One shot gets shorter, the next one longer; the picture inside the clips stays where it is.
  • A cut moves at most 1 bar, 12, 6 or 3 frames. Cuts further away stay put.
  • Where a clip has no more picture to grow into, a gap is left exactly as wide as what is missing, for you to fill. Gaps show in pink.
  • Very short runs of shots keep their own rhythm: a cut never travels more than half of the shorter shot next to it.
  • Undo last action takes the whole snap back in one step.

6 Match (experimental, Pro)

Puts a second song in time with the first one, for a mashup or a switch from one song to the other. It is tested on few songs so far.

  1. Put the second song on another audio track, select it and press Analyze second song. Its beats show in the SONG 2 lane.
  2. Speed to match tells you what to type in Premiere if the tempos differ: right-click the song › Speed/Duration, enter the number, tick Maintain Audio Pitch. A plugin cannot change speed itself. Then analyze it again.
  3. Put the playhead where the songs meet (Suggest a switch point finds a phrase start for you), choose Beat, Bar or Phrase, and press Line up at the playhead. Beats apart here shows how far apart they are. Undo moves the song back.

  • Reset to defaults.
  • Log and Copy log: what the plugin did, step by step. Send it with any bug report.

Known limits

  • Tempo: slow songs (below about 75 BPM) can be counted twice as fast. If a ballad shows 134 instead of 67, press ½×. Songs that change tempo, or have no beat, are not supported yet.
  • Sections: intros, builds and drops are found by ear-like rules and can be a beat or two off on unusual tracks. The live view always shows what was found.
  • Hard techno with a rumble under the kick: kicks may land in SNARE or BASS. Use Your sound on one kick, or set CUSTOM to about 100 to 180 Hz.
  • Playback: a plugin cannot start or stop Premiere's playback. Press the space bar in Premiere.
  • Speed: a plugin cannot change clip speed, so Match tells you the number to type.
  • Undo works within one session of the panel, up to 8 steps. Effects or keyframes on clips the plugin replaced are not brought back by it; Premiere's own Ctrl+Z still does that.
  • First SURPRISE ME! on raw clips removes what is left of them outside the edit. Undo brings them back.
  • Mac is not tested yet.

FAQ

Is it really free? Yes. The beta is pay what you like, and $0 is fine.

What happens at 1.0? 1.0 will be a paid release. Everyone who reviews the beta on Gumroad gets it free.

Will it mess up my project? It adds cuts, markers and layers to your sequence and never changes your own effects or adjustment layers. Every action is one Undo away.

Does it need the internet? No. The analysis runs on your computer.

How is it different from beat-sync tools? Beat-sync cuts on the beat grid. DROP IT! hears the accents inside the music (kicks, snares, hits, drops, your own sounds) and can build a whole first cut for you to change.
